Publication Ethics Statement

Comprehensive Geriatrics is a member of the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) and adheres to COPE's Core Practices and guidelines for publication ethics. We are committed to maintaining the highest standards of integrity, transparency, and accountability in all aspects of the publication process.

Our ethical framework is built on the following core principles:

  • Integrity: All published research must be honest, accurate, and reproducible
  • Transparency: Authors must fully disclose methodology, data, conflicts, and funding
  • Fairness: Editorial decisions are based solely on scientific merit and scope fit
  • Confidentiality: Peer review is conducted under strict confidentiality
  • Accountability: All parties (authors, reviewers, editors) are accountable for their actions

COPE Membership: As a COPE member, we follow COPE's guidelines for handling allegations of misconduct, appeals, and corrections. For more information, visit publicationethics.org.

Peer Review Policy

Comprehensive Geriatrics employs a double-blind peer review process to ensure fair and unbiased evaluation of all submissions.

Review Process Overview

  1. Initial Check — The Editorial Office screens submissions for completeness, scope alignment, formatting compliance, and plagiarism (using iThenticate).
  2. Editorial Assessment — The Editor-in-Chief or assigned Associate Editor evaluates whether the manuscript meets the journal's scope and minimum quality standards. Manuscripts that do not meet these criteria may be desk-rejected at this stage.
  3. Peer Review — Suitable manuscripts are sent to at least two independent expert reviewers. Reviewers are selected based on their expertise, publication record, and absence of conflicts of interest.
  4. Reviewer Reports — Reviewers evaluate manuscripts on: originality, methodological rigor, clinical or scientific significance, data quality, ethical compliance, and clarity of presentation.
  5. Editorial Decision — The Editor makes one of the following decisions:
    • Accept — Manuscript is accepted as submitted
    • Minor Revision — Small corrections required; typically one round
    • Major Revision — Substantive changes required; may undergo re-review
    • Reject — Manuscript is not suitable for publication
  6. Revision & Final Decision — Authors revise according to reviewer comments. The Editor makes the final decision.

Reviewer Guidelines

Reviewers are expected to:

  • Evaluate manuscripts objectively and constructively
  • Declare any conflicts of interest before accepting a review
  • Maintain confidentiality of the manuscript and review process
  • Complete reviews within the agreed timeframe (typically 14 days)
  • Report any suspected ethical issues (plagiarism, data fabrication) to the Editor

Reviewer Recognition

We publish an annual list of reviewers who have contributed to the journal as a token of appreciation. Outstanding reviewers may be invited to join the Editorial Board.

Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest (COI) exists when professional judgment concerning a primary interest (such as the validity of research) may be influenced by a secondary interest (such as financial gain or personal relationships).

Author Conflicts of Interest

All authors must disclose any financial or non-financial competing interests at the time of submission, including:

  • Employment or consulting relationships with commercial entities
  • Stock ownership or equity interests
  • Patents or patent applications (pending or actual)
  • Research funding from commercial sources
  • Personal relationships that could influence the research
  • Political or religious beliefs that could affect objectivity

If no conflicts exist, authors must state: "The authors declare no competing interests."

Reviewer Conflicts of Interest

Reviewers must decline to review manuscripts if they:

  • Are authors or co-authors of the manuscript
  • Have a close personal or professional relationship with any author
  • Have a financial interest in the research outcomes
  • Are in direct competition with the authors' research group

Editor Conflicts of Interest

Editors must recuse themselves from handling manuscripts where they have a conflict of interest. Such manuscripts are assigned to another editor. Editorial Board members may not use privileged information from submitted manuscripts for personal research.

Data Sharing Policy

Comprehensive Geriatrics encourages authors to share the data underlying their research findings to promote transparency, reproducibility, and scientific progress.

Data Availability Statement

All manuscripts must include a Data Availability Statement that specifies:

  • Where the data can be accessed (repository URL, supplementary files, etc.)
  • Any restrictions on data access and the reasons for those restrictions
  • Contact information for data access requests if data is not publicly available

Recommended Data Repositories

  • General: Figshare, Zenodo, Dryad, Harvard Dataverse
  • Clinical/Health: ClinicalStudyDataRequest.com, Yale University Open Data Access (YODA)
  • Genomic: GEO, ArrayExpress, dbGaP
  • Neuroimaging: OpenNeuro, NeuroVault

Exceptions

Data sharing may be restricted for legitimate reasons, including:

  • Participant privacy and confidentiality requirements
  • Third-party restrictions (e.g., data owned by commercial partners)
  • Indigenous data sovereignty considerations
  • National security concerns

Authors must explain any restrictions in their Data Availability Statement.

Plagiarism Detection

All submitted manuscripts are screened for plagiarism and text similarity using iThenticate by Turnitin, the leading plagiarism detection software used by major publishers worldwide.

Screening Process

  • Every submission undergoes automatic screening upon submission
  • The Editorial Office reviews similarity reports for flagged content
  • Manuscripts with significant unoriginal content are rejected
  • Self-plagiarism (substantial overlap with authors' own prior work) is also assessed

What Constitutes Plagiarism

  • Direct copying of text without quotation marks and proper attribution
  • Paraphrasing without citation of the original source
  • Reusing substantial portions of one's own published work without disclosure
  • Submitting the same manuscript to multiple journals simultaneously
  • Using figures, tables, or data from other sources without permission

Consequences

Submissions found to contain plagiarism will be:

  • Desk-rejected without further review
  • Reported to the authors' institutions if the misconduct is egregious
  • Documented in accordance with COPE guidelines

Corrections, Retractions & Expressions of Concern

Comprehensive Geriatrics is committed to maintaining the integrity of the scientific record. When errors or misconduct are discovered after publication, we take appropriate corrective action.

Correction (Erratum / Corrigendum)

A Correction is published when:

  • An error in the published article does not affect the overall conclusions
  • Author affiliations or names need updating
  • Minor typographical or formatting errors are identified

Corrections are linked to the original article and indexed accordingly.

Retraction

A Retraction is published when:

  • There is clear evidence that the findings are unreliable due to misconduct (fabrication, falsification) or honest error
  • The findings have previously been published elsewhere without proper attribution
  • The research is found to be unethical
  • There is evidence of authorship manipulation or other major breaches of publication ethics

Retractions follow COPE guidelines. The retraction notice explains the reason for retraction and is linked to the original article. The original article remains accessible but is clearly labeled as retracted.

Expression of Concern

An Expression of Concern may be issued when:

  • An investigation into potential misconduct is ongoing
  • There is inconclusive evidence of misconduct but sufficient concern to alert readers
  • The journal has been made aware of credible allegations that require investigation

Advertising Policy

Comprehensive Geriatrics may accept advertising to support open access publishing. All advertising is independent of editorial content and decisions.

Editorial Independence

  • Advertisers have no influence on editorial decisions or journal content
  • The Editorial Board operates independently from advertising and commercial interests
  • Editorial content is never influenced by advertisers or sponsors

Advertising Standards

  • All advertisements must comply with applicable laws and regulations
  • Advertising for pharmaceutical products must include all required disclosures
  • The journal reserves the right to decline any advertisement at its discretion
  • Advertisements are clearly distinguished from editorial content
